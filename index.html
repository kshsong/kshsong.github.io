Biography

Kaisheng Song, PhD. From September 2020 to June 2024 I pursued a PhD in Chemical Engineering and Technology at Chongqing University, supervised by Prof. Jun Li; from July 2024 to June 2026 I worked as a postdoctoral researcher at the School of Chemistry and Chemical Engineering, Chongqing University, with Prof. Jun Li as my co-advisor. In 2023, funded by the China Scholarship Council (CSC), I undertook joint doctoral training in the group of Prof. Markus Meuwly at the University of Basel, Switzerland. I am no longer employed and am currently seeking new opportunities.

My research focuses on gas-phase elementary reactions of small molecules, chiefly the OH + CH3OH, H2 + NH2− and H2COO systems. I have developed and applied PIP-NN, FI-NN, PhysNet and Δ-Machine Learning methods to construct full-dimensional, high-accuracy machine learning potential energy surfaces, and have studied reaction rate coefficients, reaction cross sections, product energy disposal and ro-vibrational mode specificity. This work has produced a systematic body of results on the construction of high-accuracy potential energy surfaces and the microscopic mechanisms of reaction dynamics.

I combine strong computational and method-development skills with international collaboration experience. I work with Python, Fortran, Shell/Bash and high-performance computing environments, and have developed research software including DML, PIPNN-gen and h3s-fit. My work brings together quantum chemistry, machine learning and molecular simulation to address concrete scientific questions.

Research

Computational Chemistry and Molecular Reaction Dynamics

Gas-phase reaction mechanisms of small molecules, full-dimensional high-accuracy potential energy surfaces, reaction dynamics, and ro-vibrational mode-specific dynamics.

Machine Learning Potential Energy Surfaces and AI for Science

PIP-NN, FI-NN, PhysNet, Δ-Machine Learning and machine learning molecular dynamics methods applied to complex chemical systems.

Research Workflow and Technical Approach

Starting from high-level quantum chemistry calculations, the workflow proceeds through dataset construction, development of machine learning potential energy surfaces and their validation, and finally reaction dynamics simulations and mechanistic analysis. Dynamics methods include quasi-classical trajectory (QCT) and RPMD. Core capabilities cover high-level quantum chemistry calculations, machine learning potential energy surfaces, molecular reaction dynamics and HPC parallel computing.
